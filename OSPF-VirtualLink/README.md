# 🛰️ OSPF Multi‑Area Lab with Virtual Link

## 📌 Topology Overview
This project demonstrates an **OSPF multi‑area design** with three areas:

- **Area 0 (Backbone)** → R1 ↔ R2  
- **Area 10 (Transit)** → R2 ↔ R3 ↔ R4  
- **Area 20 (Non‑backbone)** → R4 ↔ R5  

![OSPF Topology](topology/ospf-topology.png)

---

## ⚠️ Drawbacks Without Backbone Connection
- OSPF requires **all non‑backbone areas** to connect to **Area 0**.  
- In this design, **Area 20** connects only to Area 10 via R4, not directly to Area 0.  
- Consequences:
  - Routes from Area 20 are not visible in Area 0.  
  - Type‑3 Summary LSAs generated by R4 stop at Area 10.  
  - Inter‑area communication between Area 20 and other areas fails.  

---

## ✅ Solution: Virtual Link
To overcome this, a **virtual link** was configured between:
- **R2 (ABR for Area 0/10)**  
- **R4 (ABR for Area 10/20)**  
using **Area 10 as the transit area**.

This makes Area 20 logically part of the backbone, enabling full route exchange.

---

## 🔧 Configuration Syntax

### R2 (ABR: Area 0 & Area 10)
```bash
router ospf 10
 router-id 2.2.2.2
 network 10.0.0.0 0.0.0.255 area 0
 network 172.16.0.0 0.0.0.255 area 10
 area 10 virtual-link 4.4.4.4 authentication message-digest
 area 10 virtual-link 4.4.4.4 message-digest-key 1 md5 MySecureKey


### R4 (ABR: Area 10 & Area 20)
router ospf 10
 router-id 4.4.4.4
 network 172.16.0.0 0.0.0.255 area 10
 network 192.168.1.0 0.0.0.255 area 20
 area 10 virtual-link 2.2.2.2 authentication message-digest
 area 10 virtual-link 2.2.2.2 message-digest-key 1 md5 MySecureKey




🔒 Security & Performance Enhancements
OSPF Authentication
MD5 authentication enabled on the virtual link to prevent unauthorized routers from forming adjacencies.
Stronger than plaintext authentication, ensuring secure neighbor relationships.

Route Summarization
Summarize networks at ABRs to reduce LSDB size and improve convergence.

Stub/NSSA Areas
Convert Area 20 into a stub or NSSA to reduce unnecessary external LSAs.

Redundancy
Add a second ABR connection to Area 20 for high availability instead of relying solely on a virtual link.
